Audience & format

Learn by doing, on your own laptop.

This course is designed for fMRI researchers with beginning to intermediate skill levels. For newcomers, it provides a comprehensive set of foundational tools for acquiring and analyzing fMRI data. For experienced researchers, it offers advanced training in Independent Component Analysis and optimizing the validity of your studies.

The format is interactive lectures with hands-on demonstrations and work-through sessions. Participants work through examples on their own laptops. Registration is first-come, first-served, and enrollment is limited by the interactive nature of the course.

Software used in the course (install instructions and a MATLAB trial are provided to attendees):

  • MATLAB
  • SPM25
  • GIFT
  • CANlab Core Tools
  • SnPM
  • Mediation (M3) toolbox

Topics

What we cover

Fourteen threads woven across three days, each paired with a hands-on exercise.

  1. MRI physicsPulse sequences, reconstruction, and where the BOLD signal comes from
  2. Human factors and high-quality study designParticipants, stimulus presentation, behavioral monitoring
  3. Equipment and timing considerationsScanner setup, synchronization, and acquisition choices
  4. Signal processingRealignment, slice timing, unwarping, coregistration, normalization, smoothing
  5. SPM softwareHands-on with SPM25 from data checking to results
  6. General Linear Model analysisPredictors, contrasts, filtering, nuisance regressors, diagnostics, basis sets
  7. Thresholding and multiple comparisonsFixed, random and mixed effects; parametric and permutation inference
  8. Experimental design optimizationPsychological and statistical principles for efficient, valid designs
  9. Independent Component AnalysisTheory and practice of ICA for fMRI
  10. GIFT ICA softwareGroup ICA with the GIFT toolbox, step by step
  11. Resting-state connectivityIntrinsic networks and time-varying connectivity
  12. Mediation analysis and path modelingBrain mediators of behavior with the Mediation toolbox
  13. Machine learning and pattern recognitionMultivariate brain signatures with CANlab tools
  14. Reproducible analysisCoding and data-management practices that survive peer review
